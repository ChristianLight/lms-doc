# 07. Coursework, teams, uploads and peer review

Coursework uses the same attempt, response revision and final submission model as a quiz. Staff may configure constructed responses, file evidence, rubric work, peer review and resubmission. Each feature has its own eligibility and release policy. A preliminary draft, a sealed submission, marker feedback and a revised submission remain separately addressable.

## Individual coursework

A candidate reads the prompt, expected artifacts, allowed file formats, versioned rubric if released, due and close times, required declarations and permitted resubmission count. Attachments enter quarantine first. The candidate sees upload in progress, scanning, approved, rejected or detached. An attempt may keep multiple approved references where the interaction permits it. Files remain private even if the source upload originated from a shared space. File names are display metadata and cannot contain path separators or masquerade as executable content.

Use upload initiation to allocate a scoped upload record with expected media type, byte length, asset scope, interaction key and digest if supplied. Default limit is 10 files per interaction, 50 MiB per file and 200 MiB total per attempt. Candidate uploads to the issued bounded target. Complete verifies actual byte count, type detection, digest, immutable asset registration, current writer authorization and anti-malware scan status. Archive and active formats require separately reviewed capabilities and scanning. Candidate cannot seal pending files by selecting their internal asset IDs. Rejected files are excluded with accessible reason and replacement path. Candidate replaces a file by a new immutable asset revision and a new response revision. An already sealed original remains accessible under policy.

A teacher returning a draft before submission may attach guidance while the draft stays editable. A teacher reviewing sealed evidence can award marks, return feedback without marks, request authorized new attempt or approve a new revision through the disposition workflow. Return-for-revision creates an explicit grant with additional quota, instructions and new hard deadline. The new attempt has its own form and receipt and points to the previous attempt. If policy carries previous answers into the new draft, create new ResponseRevisions with source references under the new scope. Candidate confirms and can edit. Changing the form must not copy values whose schemas changed. Academic records select latest, best or an explicit attempt according to their policy.

## Group work and team identity

A learning group is a roster object. TeamSnapshot is evidence of assessment participation. Freeze its team member Enrollment and EnrollmentRelease IDs, role and allowed actions, scoped learning group and membership revision, plus active eligibility at start. The exact course version of every member release must contain the same delivery placement. By default a learner participates in one stable team per delivery. Repeated authorized sittings of that team create separate immutable member snapshot links. Lock membership insertion when starting the team attempt. Candidate from another group cannot write by guessing the attempt ID. Later group changes do not silently add editors, erase credited members or move already sealed work. A moderation decision can add or exclude a member's award only through a separate reviewed disposition with reason and approval.

Default team roles are editor and observer. Explicit submitter role can restrict finalization to designated members. All editors can see who last saved a field and whether drafts are current. Use per-field concurrency and separate leases for each editor. For a formal group submission, the candidate finalizer signs a declaration and the receipt records that actor and captured team members. Optional all-member acknowledgement stores per-member policy acknowledgement before sealing. A departed member's institutional access may be revoked even if the member remains in historical evidence. Staff can inspect the membership change and the snapshot separately.

A team can earn 80/100 with three eligible members E1, E2 and E3. The accepted team mark emits one native evidence revision for each enrollment. A separately approved individual adjustment to E3 changes only E3's evidence to 72/100 and preserves the underlying team mark. An observer who was never eligible for credit generates no evidence. Replaying the team mark event cannot append duplicate records.

## Peer and self review

PeerReviewPolicyVersion states candidate pool, eligible submissions, minimum completed reviews, required outgoing reviews, review window, allocation algorithm version, reviewer conflicts, rubric version, moderation requirements and fallback. Default peer feedback is formative and has zero grade effect. A graded mode requires independent approval, configured aggregation and fallback to staff when evidence is insufficient. A staff reference rubric can be kept private until peer reviews are sealed. Self review is a distinct attempt role and has no authority to replace staff marks.

PeerReviewAllocationRun freezes all eligible reviewer enrollment IDs and sealed submission IDs at a cutoff. Exclude same person, same frozen team, conflicts declared before cutoff, blocked reviewer-submission pairs, previous assignment when repeated exposure is forbidden, and audience mismatches. One peer cannot review the same sealed submission more than once in a round. Default requires two incoming and two outgoing reviews where feasible, no self or team review and deterministic tie-breaking. Scope incoming and outgoing limits separately and permit a reviewer to receive fewer assignments if conflicts block allocation. Reciprocal pairs may be prohibited by a reviewed policy. Keep this constraint explicit because it can make a small cohort impossible.

Use a min-cost max-flow allocation. Source→reviewer edges have capacity for available outgoing tasks. Reviewer→submission allowed edges have capacity 1 and deterministic cost after sorting by a stable seed. Submission→sink has one edge for each permitted incoming position. The first edge costs 0, the second costs a large fixed penalty and so on, encouraging coverage before concentration. Choose penalty larger than sum of tie-breaking costs for the run. Maximize flow, then minimize coverage cost and stable rank tie-breaks. Report any target below the required minimum. A no-reciprocals option requires a post-validation and bounded repair step that removes at least one edge in each reciprocal pair and reruns allocation with a recorded exclusion. If repair fails, route unmet cases to staff instead of accepting invalid allocations. All assignments and fallback cases commit with a run manifest. Retrying with same run and roster digest yields identical assignments.

For reviewer R1 allowed A or B, R2 only A and R3 only C with one required review each, the complete assignment is R1→B, R2→A and R3→C. If R3 becomes ineligible before committing, A and B remain allocatable and C receives a staff fallback case. If R3 withdraws after receiving an assignment, revoke their review access and create a replacement assignment against the same sealed evidence, with the old allocation and reason preserved.

PeerReviewAssignment tracks invited, opened, submitted and revoked. Reads derive expired for an invited or opened review at or after its exclusive cutoff. Reviewer receives only the scoped sanitized submission representation and pseudonymous label. Remove author names, original file metadata and comments that could identify the learner where institutional policy promises anonymity. Where free text or shared project context makes deanonymization possible, explain this limitation in the workflow and disable automatic disclosure on flagged evidence. Staff verify abuse and identity inference reports. A learner sees only approved feedback and their own assignment tasks. Peer allocation event payloads omit reviewer-to-candidate identity mapping.

A peer reviewer completes a private rubric draft, gets accessible validation and submits an immutable PeerReviewRevision. Record exact evidence and rubric. For graded peer marking, the default contribution if enabled is the median per criterion across at least three valid peer marks, with an even-count median defined as the average of middle values. Staff marks supersede peer contribution where conflict or insufficient coverage is recorded. With peer criterion scores 1, 4, 4 and 5, the median is 4. A missing review does not mark the reviewed student zero. A reviewer engagement grade, if used, is a separate assessed line and never alters the recipient's result.

## Survey anonymity

Identified or confidential surveys store responses under attempt scope and can emit completion. A truly anonymous survey uses an eligibility redemption separate from response storage. Issue a one-use unlinkable token through an approved design. Persist only redemption and eligibility completion on the identity side. Store anonymous submissions without candidate ID, device ID, matching timestamp, raw token or trace ID on the response side. Delay or bucket analytics to reduce inference and set a small-group threshold. Explain that authored free text can reveal identity. An anonymous survey never yields person-linked score or review.

## Staff and learner acceptance checks

- An upload approved after hard close remains a staged asset and is absent from the already sealed manifest.
- A student leaving a group cannot remove earlier evidence or retain write rights automatically. Team membership for past credit is still shown to authorized staff.
- A reviewer withdrawal causes exactly one replacement or staff fallback. The replacement receives the original sealed evidence and rubric.
- A rubric submission cannot be edited after finalization. Reopening review creates a new revision with audit history.
- Cross-team and self-review edges are absent, and the reviewer cannot inspect another assignment by changing an ID.
- Screen-reader and keyboard users can upload, detach, confirm group submission and file a peer feedback flag without drag-and-drop.
